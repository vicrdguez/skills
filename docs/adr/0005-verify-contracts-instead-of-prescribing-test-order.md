# Verify contracts instead of prescribing test order

The Workflow will judge behavioral conformance, architectural conformance, and local implementation quality separately, replacing mandatory test-first sequencing and one-scenario/one-test cycles with contract-grounded verification. Implementors may choose construction order, test organization, and appropriate existing verification boundaries while preserving required observations, agreed architecture, and repository standards. Independent Watchdog Review may block concrete violations, material risks, or specific evidence gaps, not alternative preferences; meaningful nonblocking shortcomings may become Debt Markers. This is an explicit redesign of agent behavior beyond ADR 0004's compatibility-first extraction, not a transfer of judgment into the Workflow Engine.

## Status

Accepted after final Explore confirmation; implementation is pending. This record does not change active Skill Definitions or previously published Implementation Ledgers.

## Consequences

- Test-first ordering is optional for features and behavior-preserving changes. TDD remains an available technique, not an acceptance criterion or required coordination structure.
- Bug fixes require demonstrated regression sensitivity regardless of writing order: the check detects the reported wrong behavior and passes with the fix. Early reproduction is encouraged. When the original failure cannot be reproduced reliably or safely, a faithful isolated reproduction, captured-trace replay, or controlled fault injection may establish protection if it preserves the relevant trigger and observable failure; the Submission must state what remains unverified. Without credible evidence for a material failure, seek a human decision rather than claim verification or silently carry the uncertainty as debt.
- One accountable implementor may delegate and parallelize non-conflicting testing and implementation work within its claimed Work Item. A separate test-writing agent is not mandatory, and this does not authorize additional Claims or weaken final verification of the integrated result.
- Final Explore confirmation is the semantic approval of consequential rules, architectural commitments, and delegated choices. Propose must check materialization against those decisions and return to the human for material changes, contradictions, or unresolved interpretations rather than require routine rereading of every artifact.
- Before publication, Propose uses one bounded fresh-context fidelity review across the proposed slices, supplying the approved Explore recap and referenced decisions explicitly. The reviewer identifies omitted, weakened, strengthened, contradictory, or invented obligations without redesigning the solution or reopening accepted choices. Propose may correct demonstrable transcription errors; actual decision gaps and semantic changes return to the human.
- Unspecified implementation details are delegated only when the alternatives preserve accepted behavior, architecture, and mandatory standards. An unambiguously implied case may be implemented and tested without extending the frozen scenario list. A consequential behavioral or architectural decision not resolved by the contract requires human resolution, not an assumption that silence grants permission; neither implementor nor reviewer may invent additional requirements.
- Implementation and Audit assess the changed test set as a whole, reusing coverage, strengthening assertions, and consolidating or removing tests only while required behavioral and failure-mode protection remains intact. Removed or weakened assertions receive scrutiny, but no per-test justification ledger or unique-bug quota is required. Unrequested whole-repository test pruning is out of scope.
- In-scope refactoring is permitted throughout implementation while preserving architectural obligations and regression protection; Audit remains the final quality and conformance pass. Before the submission's Audit, on both first implementation and finding-driven Rework, the implementor merges the current integration target (main in this repository) into the work branch and resolves conflicts. Integration and conflict resolution belong before the normal review, not in an additional review pass merely because conflicts existed; merging the Submission into its destination remains human-owned.
- Observe and integrate the target once immediately before that submission's Audit, recording the integrated target SHA in the existing verification evidence. Later target movement alone does not restart the round. Evidence must cover the submitted result: further functional edits require affected checks and a Full Gate covering the final functional state; another target merge requires review of its integration effects and verification of the resulting state rather than reuse of stale evidence. Rework review accounts for integration and conflict-resolution effects without reopening settled preferences or treating unrelated upstream additions as scope creep.
- Watchdog reviews every obligation; additional executable challenges are driven by concrete risk or uncertainty. Neither a universal mutation-score target nor a duplicate test suite is required, and prose alone does not replace credible automated checks for ordinary executable behavior.
- Explicit obligations in published Implementation Ledgers remain binding. New freedom applies where those artifacts do not constrain it, and new artifact conventions apply to future Proposals; material conflicts require an explicit human disposition through the existing Workflow rather than ledger rewriting or automatic migration. No new versioning or compatibility mechanism is required by this decision.
- Keep the existing artifact filenames: intent.md states the desired result, scope, exclusions, Definition of Done, and Manual Verification without duplicating detailed behavior; behavior.md contains scoped named rules and binding discriminating scenarios; plan.md is required for pinned architectural decisions and records responsibility ownership, boundary assumptions, interface commitments, and verification strategy while referencing existing ADRs; tasks.md exists only when sequencing, dependencies, or coordination help, not to duplicate scenarios.
- Require explicit rules where scenarios alone leave consequential ambiguity, not redundant wrappers for every simple behavior. Stateful-behavior questions are precision aids, not compulsory boilerplate. Use readable, uniquely referenceable headings rather than a requirement database or mandatory ID scheme; only deliberately agreed interface details are contractual, not incidental choices in proposal-generated sketches.
- The Submission's existing Verification section groups many-to-many mappings from all rules, scenarios, and architectural obligations to concrete tests, commands, or appropriate inspection evidence, with results and material limitations. Existing checks may cover several obligations, and an obligation may need several kinds of evidence. Every obligation must be accounted for, but not in a separate row; listing an evidence gap does not make it acceptable.
- Replace the tdd skill with one testing skill covering observable behavior, appropriate verification boundaries, independent expected outcomes, regression sensitivity, mocking, and retained test value, without prescribing sequential red-green construction. TDD remains a technique an agent may choose or a human may request; the testing skill need not advertise it explicitly. Installed stubs and resource references must transition coherently while respecting existing published obligations. Acceptance and finding criteria remain with Audit and are available to Watchdog without rerunning Audit.
- No agent-behavior evaluation, benchmark, or new evaluation framework is required for this redesign. Use normal project checks and focused verification of changed instruction distribution and resource retrieval; improve the guidance through observed usage rather than claim those checks prove agent behavior.
- A late change in mergeability alone does not invalidate a successful Watchdog verdict at its reviewed revision or initiate another automatic Rework/review cycle. Ready for Merge remains distinct from conflict-free integration. Late integration and merging the Submission into its destination remain human-owned; historical approval of the inputs does not itself establish review of the conflict-resolution result. Reuse the separate human-integration work in #37/#46 rather than introduce competing conflict-driven routing here.
- The existing Full Gate, independent Watchdog Review, and human merge boundary remain. A human-invoked integration-only Implement mode and post-approval conflict-resolution automation are explicitly out of scope and deferred to separate work. Pre-Audit integration and its single-snapshot cutoff remain in scope.
